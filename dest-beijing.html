Culture & History

Why visit Beijing?

China's capital for most of the past eight centuries, Beijing is where imperial grandeur meets modern ambition. The Forbidden City, the Great Wall, the Temple of Heaven — these are icons the world knows. But the city's real magic hides in its hutong alleyways, its dawn tai chi gatherings, and its dumpling restaurants that have changed nothing in 150 years.

Must-See Sights

Iconic experiences in Beijing

The Forbidden City

The world's largest palace complex — 980 buildings, 500 years of emperors, and golden roofs that glow at dusk. Our scholar-guides bring 600 years of drama to life.

The Great Wall at Mutianyu

Skip the tourist crowds at Badaling. We take you to a quieter, fully restored section at dawn — you may have the wall to yourself for an hour.

Temple of Heaven

Where Ming emperors prayed for good harvests. Arrive early to see locals practicing tai chi, singing opera, and flying kites.

The Hutongs

Cycle or walk through Beijing's ancient courtyard neighborhoods. Visit a local family for a home-cooked dumpling dinner.

Recommended experiences

  • Peking duck carved tableside, the 1872 way

Best Season
April–May and September–October offer the most comfortable weather. Winters are cold but uncrowded; summers are hot and humid.
Local Flavors
Peking duck, zhajiangmian, jiaozi dumplings, tanghulu candied hawthorn, and the capital's legendary street breakfasts.
Where to Stay
From heritage courtyard hotels in the hutongs to ultra-modern suites of the Park Hyatt.
